HOW IT WORKS

Four steps. One working day.

1 · Brief

You describe the task (contracts, e-mail, documentation, support…) and the constraints: on-prem, language, budget, data sensitivity.

2 · Sample

We build representative tasks: synthetic, public, or from a sample you anonymise. Your real documents stay with you.

3 · Measurement

Candidate models run on a rented GPU (80 GB). The run takes minutes; afterwards the machine is destroyed automatically — even if anything fails.

4 · Result

A table, a recommendation, and a measurement record you can check afterwards: what was measured, on what, with what result.

EXAMPLE · PILOT FOR A LAW FIRM · ANONYMISED

Which open model can handle Czech legal documents?

Seven tasks: extracting parties, deadlines and penalties from contracts, summarising a judgment, working across several documents, a statute question — and one task where the correct answer is “it isn't in the documents”. All data was synthetic.

What followed: the largest model (72B) added nothing on these tasks for roughly twice the memory. The 27B model passed everything and fits a 32 GB card, with a healthy margin up to 48 GB. The fastest, 24B, failed one task out of seven. Measuring all three models took 11 minutes on a single A100 80 GB; compute cost under one dollar.

What needs saying before anyone asks

  • It is not a production load test with hundreds of concurrent users; we measure the model and the task, not your whole application.
  • Results hold for the measured configuration and task sample. Seven pilot tasks is a small sample; for an engagement the set is built with you and is usually larger.
  • We never measure your real data on the rented machine. Measuring on live data is only possible on your own hardware.
  • We don't sell hardware or model licences — so we have no reason to recommend anything bigger than you need.
